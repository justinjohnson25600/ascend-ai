## Who Ascend AI is

Ascend AI (Business Automation Solutions) builds bespoke AI automation for UK owner-run small businesses, typically with 2 to 25 staff, in service sectors heavy on admin: trades, clinics and salons, agencies and consultancies, professional services, hospitality, and shops or online stores.

Tagline: Run your business on autopilot, not overtime.

Business automation is AI quietly taking the repetitive, time-consuming jobs off an owner's plate: reading and answering email, chasing quotes, filing receipts, booking customers in and reminding them to turn up. It works in the business's own words and by its rules, around the clock, for a fraction of the cost of another pair of hands, giving the owner the hours back for the work only they can do.

Why now: software used to need expensive systems and a consultant, so automation stayed with big companies. AI can now read an email and understand what is being asked, not just move data from one box to another, which makes automation practical for a business of three people.

What makes Ascend AI different:
- Bespoke. It is built around how the business already works, not a package or template the business has to fit.
- It works with the software the business already uses (email, calendar, accounts package, website, booking and messaging tools). If something genuinely cannot be connected to, that comes out at the audit.
- Delivered in stages, so something useful is live early, usually within weeks for a first stage.
- Kept running, monitored and improved by Ascend AI.
- The client owns what is built for them.

What Ascend AI does not do: sell a package and make the business fit it; replace software the business likes; put an AI in front of customers that makes things up (it answers from the business's own information or hands over to a person); build something and disappear.

## What it automates

- Enquiries and follow-up: every enquiry from the website, email, social pages or phone messages gets an immediate reply in the business's tone of voice, is qualified, booked in for a call or visit, and followed up until there is an answer. The owner sees every conversation and still makes the decision.
- Quotes and invoicing: quotes drafted from the business's own price list and past jobs, sent for approval or straight to the customer, chased politely on a set schedule; invoices sent and chased when the job is marked done, inside the accounts software already in use. The owner keeps pricing rules and approval of anything unusual.
- Scheduling and reminders: customers book, move and cancel within the owner's rules; confirmations and reminders by text or email; staff diaries and job lists kept in step; no-shows followed up.
- Customer questions: an assistant on the website, email or messaging channels that answers only from the business's own information (prices, hours, policies, product details) and hands anything else to a person with the conversation attached.
- Paperwork and data entry: information captured once, from a form, email, photo of a document or call note, checked and pushed to every system that needs it. Receipts photographed or forwarded are entered into the accounts, categorised and matched to the bank, with gaps such as a payment with no receipt flagged to the owner.
- Reporting: the handful of numbers the owner runs the business on, sent on the schedule they choose, for example jobs booked, quotes outstanding, cash due and hours by person.
- A missed call can get an automatic text back within a minute asking what the caller needs, then book them in.

Typical first stages by kind of business (typical, not fixed):
- A trade: missed calls texted back and booked in; quotes drafted from the price list and chased; receipts photographed on site and filed into the accounts; review requests sent when a job is marked done.
- A clinic or salon: online booking with deposits and reminders; no-shows followed up and rebooked; intake and consent forms completed before the appointment; answers to the questions reception gets all day.
- An agency or consultancy: enquiries qualified and booked into discovery calls; new clients onboarded with agreement, forms and folders set up; timesheets and project updates collected without chasing; overdue invoices chased.
- A café, pub or restaurant: table and event enquiries answered and booked; review replies drafted for approval; supplier orders built from stock counts; rota questions answered from the rota.
- A shop or online store: "where is my order?" answered from the order system; returns handled to the shop's policy; low stock flagged; a daily sales summary sent to the owner's phone.

## How it works and what it costs

1. The free audit: a 30 minute call about how the business runs and where the time goes. Within two working days the visitor gets a short written document, theirs to keep whether or not they go ahead: how the business runs today, where the hours go, what could be automated first, what each piece would involve, and a rough cost range. No obligation.
2. Scope and setup: if they go ahead, the first stage is scoped in detail (what it does, what it connects to, cost, timescale). A setup fee covers access to their systems, hosting and groundwork.
3. Build in stages: each stage has a fixed development fee agreed before it starts. It is built, tested with real data and switched on, and used for real before the next stage is scoped. Some businesses do one stage, some do several over a year. The client decides the pace.
4. Run and improve: once live, Ascend AI hosts it, monitors it and fixes it when a supplier changes their system or the business changes its rules, for an ongoing monthly fee. Improvements and new stages are quoted as they come up.

Fees in short: a setup fee once at the start; a fixed development fee per stage; a monthly ongoing fee once something is live. Exact figures depend on the project; the audit document gives a range before anyone commits. No long contracts: either side can end the monthly service with 30 days' notice.

What the client provides: an hour or two per stage, mostly at the start; access to the systems involved, set up securely and revocable at any time; someone to try it in real use.

Common questions:
- Will this replace my staff? Usually it replaces evenings and weekends, not people.
- Who owns it? The client owns the automation built for them. If they move elsewhere it is handed over with documentation.
- What happens when something breaks? It is monitored, so problems are usually spotted first; fixes are covered by the monthly fee. A rebuild forced by a supplier's change is quoted separately and agreed first.
- How long does a stage take? It depends on what it does and how many systems it touches; most first stages are measured in weeks, not months.
